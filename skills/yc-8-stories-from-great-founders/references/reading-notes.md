### Mark Zuckerberg — Facebook
- **Product preceded the business**: Built the first version as a directory he and his friends wanted at Harvard; didn't think it would be a company
- **Don't commit to a company until it's working**: By his rough recollection, Facebook became a formal Delaware company about six months in, when Peter Thiel invested; he took semester-by-semester leaves from Harvard rather than dropping out at once
- **Followed demand**: Within weeks about two-thirds of Harvard students used it, and students at MIT and other schools asked for it; they expanded to more schools, then high schools, then beyond schools
- **Learning organization**: Treats the company as a learning organism run like the scientific method; engineers can ship experiments to 10,000-100,000 people, so tens of thousands of versions run at once
- **Founder conviction under pressure**: Turned down Yahoo's roughly $1B offer (summer 2006, about 10 million users); poor communication of the mission meant the whole management team left within about a year

### Anne Wojcicki — 23andMe
- **Non-obvious founder background**: Spent 10 years on Wall Street covering healthcare (biotech, pharma, hospital systems, insurers) and came to see the system as broken
- **Steadfast mission**: Her 2007 Series A documents and OKRs (captioned "okay ours") are remarkably similar to today's; an investor called her "a broken record"
- **Regulation**: Moving from unregulated to regulated was a major transition; regulators care about public safety and "speak in data," and the onus is on the startup to explain itself
- **Direct-to-consumer data as research engine**: About 5 million customers make a research machine whose discoveries go back to customers, plus a drug discovery arm
- **People in control of their own health**: Doctors pushed back on direct-to-consumer testing; her aim is data that lets people make their own health decisions
