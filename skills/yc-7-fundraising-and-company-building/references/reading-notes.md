### How Startup Fundraising Works (Brad Flora)
- **Framed as seven myths**: Fundraising is a grind of one-on-one coffee chats and Zoom calls, not Shark Tank. Freshpaint met 160 investors and 39 said yes (he calls that a very high conversion rate); checks ranged $5K-$200K, and raising $1.6M took about four and a half months
- **Build before raising**: The best founders, he says, build a first version and get some users before thinking about raising money
- **Seed rounds are small and fast**: Typically $500K to a couple million, closed in weeks or days without legal fees. A SAFE (created by YC in 2013) has only a few terms — amount, valuation cap, discount — and "nobody does discounts." Check the lecture date and current primary sources before describing prevailing terms.
- **Control**: SAFEs carry no board seats or information rights, so founders keep control; he argues bootstrapping forever is hard and proposes defining it as stretching the pain of fundraising across the company's life
- **No fancy network needed**: Investors care about making money; talk to investors yourself and turn would-be intermediaries into introductions
- **Rejection is normal**: Envision was rejected more than 50 times before its first check; Whatnot raised a fraction of its seed target

### Key Terms (Dalton Caldwell)
- **Seed vs. Series A**: In a seed round "anything goes"; a Series A usually (not always) has a lead investor, may involve a board seat, and traditionally the lead might get something like 20% ownership
- **SAFE vs. convertible note**: A convertible note is debt-like (interest, possible repayment); a SAFE has fewer terms. Read the fine print of either
- **Bootstrapping**: Raising no venture capital; entirely within your control, and a much better fit for businesses expected to reach roughly $5-10M a year in revenue rather than venture scale

### How to Apply and Succeed at YC (Dalton Caldwell)
- **What reviewers ask**: Who are the founders and how do they know each other? What exists today? Is there evidence people want it? Are the founders serious? What is impressive or unique? Weak applications obfuscate
- **Interview odds**: Probably the biggest variable is technical talent: teams with at least one founder hireable into a technical role at a top YC company have about 5x better interview odds (per the 2023 talk); non-technical teams on unlaunched tarpit ideas have the lowest odds
- **Application tips**: Fill out everything, follow the video directions, answer clearly and concisely. Misrepresenting revenue or traction is disqualifying; extraordinary claims need evidence
- **The YC interview**: A 10-minute Zoom with all founders and usually 2-4 people from YC; basic, context-dependent questions. Not adversarial — don't recite memorized speeches
- **When to apply**: "There's honestly no such thing as too early," and without product-market fit you're likely not too far along
- **What YC provides (per the 2023 talk)**: Half a million dollars up front, one-on-one office hours with group partners, and internal tools such as fundraising data
