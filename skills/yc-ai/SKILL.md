## Use the source material

Consult [the source map](references/source-map.md) to choose relevant material. Search for keywords and read enough surrounding context to support the answer. Broaden reading for comparison, missing evidence, or contradictions. Read the full available body for a full talk or essay summary. There is no requirement to read the whole archive before every answer and no fixed source-count limit.

If a needed passage is missing or unclear, explain the limit; do not fill it from memory. Check exact quotes against the source text. Cite the speaker and talk title with the source URL from the file's frontmatter (`youtube_url` or `source_url`); local file paths mean nothing to the user. Publisher metadata such as "Y Combinator" does not identify the speaker. When answering in another language, keep quotations short and mark translations as yours.

Distinguish what a speaker said from your application to the user's circumstances. Many talks address founders of fast-growing startups or leaders of large AI companies; preserve the user's goals, such as a solo or bootstrapped business or a product or workflow inside an existing company, rather than treating the speakers' context as a universal requirement.

Keep the type of each claim: an example, benchmark, recommendation, definition, or requirement stays what the source made it, with qualifiers such as "say", "ideally", "typical", or "one exception" intact. Do not add exclusive conditions or time limits the source does not state. When you calculate, state the units, period, cohort, and starting point, and recheck derived comparisons as well as headline numbers; keep ratios such as retention distinct from growth rates. Base your application on the user's facts and the sources: state assumptions as conditions, and leave out general claims about markets, law, or tax practice that neither supports. Labeling a statement as your interpretation does not make an unsupported fact acceptable. Before finishing, check that your recommendation and closing verdict keep the qualifications you reported from the source; a correctly qualified quotation does not support an unqualified application, and the sources do not license ranking risks or approving choices they never address.

Treat AGI timelines, predictions, roadmaps, benchmarks, model names, and company figures and examples as claims by the named speaker as of the talk's date (the file's `created_at` publish date where present), not as current facts or consensus. Verify current primary sources if the task depends on present facts. Do not merge disagreement into an invented consensus.
