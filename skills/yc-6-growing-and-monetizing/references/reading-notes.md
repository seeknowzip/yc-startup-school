### Setting KPIs (Divya Bhat)
- **Primary KPI**: For the vast majority of startups, growth — ideally revenue growth once launched (pre-launch: e.g., weeks until launch or user conversations; exceptions she names include marketplace signups or GMV, LOIs for long enterprise sales cycles, and hard tech such as hardware or biotech, ~26:15). Don't optimize two hard things at once
- **Secondary KPIs**: 3-5 is reasonable (e.g., retention/churn, unit economics, CAC/payback period) to keep the primary KPI honest or give an early signal when it lags. Super Daily switched from signups to customers with 5+ orders as a leading indicator
- **How much growth is enough**: Says it depends on your business and stage, then cites Paul Graham's essay: for a company going through YC, 5-7% week over week is good and 10% is exceptional
- **Prioritization**: You'll never finish your task list; work only on the biggest blocker to your primary KPI. Set targets top-down and bottom-up
- **CAC:LTV**: Usually a post-PMF concern; early on, watch payback period instead

### Key Startup Metrics (Tom Blomfield)
- **Pick 4-5 key metrics before launch**: Write down shared definitions and keep them consistent (don't switch weekly to monthly actives to look better)
- **Revenue first**: Revenue is the key metric he suggests for most B2B companies (early consumer companies may prioritize active-user growth); GMV, gross transaction value, page views, and impressions are vanity metrics. Put revenue, burn rate, and runway at the top of investor updates
- **Retention**: Cohort curves should flatten (flattening at all matters more than the level). Net dollar retention compares a cohort's revenue a year later with its starting revenue; for early-stage B2B SaaS it should be well above 100% (125-150% would be great), while 110-120% is pretty good for mature companies
- **Gross margin** (revenue minus cost of goods sold): He advises not scaling while unit economics are negative — fix them first, or at least have a plan to (Monzo scaled at a loss with one)
- **PMF signal** (David Lieb, cohort retention): If retention curves don't flatten, you probably haven't made something people want yet

### Business Models and Pricing (Aaron Epstein)
- **Nine business models**: SaaS, transactional, marketplace, hard tech, usage-based, enterprise, advertising, e-commerce, bio. SaaS is 31% of YC's top 100; five of the top 10 are marketplaces; advertising and e-commerce barely register
- **Retention compounds**: At 95% monthly retention, 54 of 100 customers remain after a year; at 90%, 28. You cannot scale negative margins
- **Revenue quality**: Recurring > one-time; predictable, with higher lifetime value and lower acquisition cost
- **Pricing**: Most startups undercharge. Find the right order of magnitude, price on value not cost, and raise prices until customers complain but still pay. Pricing isn't permanent; raising it is relatively painless (grandfather existing customers or give notice)
