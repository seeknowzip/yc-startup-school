# Reading notes

Imported module summaries retained as topic-finding aids. They are not transcripts or independently verified quotations. Read the linked original source before attributing a claim; treat numbers, rankings, and prescriptions as contextual hypotheses until checked. Do not load these notes for every question.

- **Resilience over credentials** (Harj Taggar): School and work success, and projected confidence, mattered less than expected. Benchling case — Sajith, a softly spoken engineer funded in 2012, grew into the CEO of a company worth over $6B (as of the 2022 talk) by pushing through years of sales rejection
- **Worst-case analysis**: "What do I have to lose?" as a practical, not rhetorical, question. It will probably take at least a year to have enough data to tell if the startup is promising; worst case is shutting down after that year having earned little or no salary
- **Learning as hidden asset**: Startup experience can improve career options even if the startup fails (Rippling's Parker Conrad: "probably around 50 former founders" work there)
- **Initial motivations don't matter much**: Money and curiosity are fine starting motivations; what sustains you is enduring motivation — interest in the problem and the people you work with
- **Energy differential**: A draining day job versus energizing evening/weekend side projects is a sign it may be time to quit and work on your own ideas
- **Users who love it beat tricks** (Before the Startup): A founder who has made something users love will raise money more easily than one who knows every fundraising trick but has a flat usage graph
- **PG's counterintuitive points** (Before the Startup): startup instincts mislead, but trust your instincts about people; be an expert on users, not on startups; gaming the system stops working; startups are all-consuming; you can't tell in advance whether you're up to it; to get ideas and cofounders, learn about things that matter, work on problems that interest you, with people you like and respect
