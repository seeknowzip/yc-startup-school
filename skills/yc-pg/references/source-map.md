# Source map

Select by the question, then search and read the relevant original with surrounding context. This index is a navigation aid, not evidence of the listed interpretations. Each file is a Paul Graham essay as republished in the YC Library; its frontmatter links the YC Library page and the original paulgraham.com essay, and the essay text begins with its original publication date. The frontmatter `description` is YC Library publisher text, not Graham's words. Essays span 2005-2020, so check the date before applying details such as YC terms or growth benchmarks.

[default-alive-or-default-dead.md](default-alive-or-default-dead.md) — (2015) What PG almost always asks first of a startup that has been operating more than 8 or 9 months: assuming expenses stay constant and revenue keeps growing at its rate of the last several months, does it reach profitability on the money it has left? (The test projects the current trajectory, so a company not yet profitable can be default alive; he says the question doesn't make sense to ask early on.)

9. [startup-growth.md](startup-growth.md) — (2012) A startup is a company designed to grow fast; being newly founded doesn't make a company one, and it need not work on technology, take venture funding, or have an exit. Growth rate as a compass, measured weekly during YC (5-7% a week good, 10% exceptional, 1% a sign of not yet having figured it out — all for the YC period), preferably on revenue, or active users if not charging

11. [the-equity-equation.md](the-equity-equation.md) — (2007) A test for trading stock for money, a hire, or a deal: giving up fraction n is worth it if the trade raises the company's average outcome by more than a factor of 1/(1 - n) (e.g., 7% needs more than a 7.5% improvement)
12. [the-hardest-lessons-for-startups-to-learn.md](the-hardest-lessons-for-startups-to-learn.md) — (2006) Seven lessons PG has to keep repeating, from releasing early to not getting your hopes up
