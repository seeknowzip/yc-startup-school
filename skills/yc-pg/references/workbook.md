# Guided workbook

Read only for a requested worksheet, guided practice, or diagnostic. Use the questions as prompts, adapting to the user's goals, stage, and already supplied evidence. Provide the full worksheet when requested; for interactive practice ask a manageable question and wait, using an available interface or ordinary chat. Offering a few answer options can make a question easier to answer. After each answer, respond briefly and connect it to the relevant essay before moving on. Skip irrelevant or answered prompts and respect requests to stop.

Ground feedback in relevant original passages. Separate the user's answers from your interpretation; describe supported strengths, uncertainties, and a useful next step. Score only when requested and explain the rubric and uncertainty. Do not infer character or competence from missing answers.

Save only when the user requests a file. Use their destination; if none is specified for an explicit save request, use a `yc-startup-school-notes/` folder in the current working directory with a descriptive dated filename and preserve existing files. Otherwise finish in conversation. Record partial work as partial.

## Question bank

1. Where did your idea come from — did it grow out of your own experience, or did you think it up by reasoning about what might work? ("How to Get Startup Ideas": organic vs. made-up ideas)
2. Are there ideas you have passed over because of the tedious or messy work (schleps) they would involve — or because they seemed unsexy? Would any be worth a second look? ("How to Get Startup Ideas": the schlep filter keeps people from noticing ideas because they unconsciously shrink from the schleps involved; the unsexy filter keeps them from problems they despise)
3. If you have been operating long enough for this to apply, are you default alive or default dead? Assuming expenses stay constant and revenue keeps growing at its rate of the last several months, do you reach profitability on the money you have left? Being unprofitable today does not by itself make you default dead. ("Default Alive or Default Dead": PG asks this of startups operating more than 8 or 9 months and says it doesn't make sense to ask early on)
4. If fast growth is your goal, what is your growth rate — of revenue, or active users if you are not charging — and how often do you measure it? Use available measurements and state what is unknown. ("Startup = Growth": YC measures weekly during the batch, where 5-7% a week is good; these benchmarks are for that period, not a test for every business)
5. Do any of the 18 mistakes apply to your situation — and if so, which one most? ("The 18 Mistakes That Kill Startups")
6. Over the past month, where were you relentlessly resourceful — adapting plans on the fly to get what you wanted — and where were you hapless, letting circumstances push you around? ("Be Relentlessly Resourceful")
