### The Best Way to Launch Your Startup (Kat Mañalac)
- **Launch early and often**: Launch ASAP; if no one cares, iterate and launch again (Airbnb launched three times). A few users who love you beat many who are semi-happy (Paul Buchheit)
- **Launch types**: Silent launch (landing page with a call to action) / friends and family (don't stay there long) / strangers (DoorDash interviewed 200+ small business owners) / online communities (Bookface, Show HN — Robinhood's waitlist hit #1 on HN) / social media / pre-order / waitlist. Press is hard to land early (for companies that haven't raised about $1M+, at least in the U.S.) and not a scalable way to get users
- **One-sentence pitch**: Lead with what, not why; no jargon or marketing speak; no rambling. Make it easy for anyone — your grandfather, someone at the airport — to repeat
- **"X for Y" construction**: Have a one-liner without it; if you use it, X should be a household name, it should be reasonably clear why Y might want X, and Y should be a huge market
- **Each channel reaches a different audience**: Launching on different channels shows how people respond and whether you're talking to the right users

### How to Get Your First Customers (Gustaf Alströmer)
- **Founders make startups take off**: Recruit customers manually, and learn to do sales yourself before hiring a sales team
- **Sales funnel**: Make a list → email/LinkedIn → demo → pricing → close → onboard. Track conversion in a simple CRM and work backwards from the goal (in his example, 500 outreach messages yield 2 customers)
- **First customers should be the easiest**: Your network; startups (short decision lines); drop slow-moving leads. Most people aren't early adopters, so send more messages
- **Charge from the start**: In B2B, prefer a money-back guarantee or opt-out over free trials
- **Example**: Brex recruited its first 10 customers from its YC batch with a simple virtual card and founder-led onboarding. Keep sales emails to 6-8 sentences, plain text, with a clear call to action

### Do Things That Don't Scale (Paul Graham)
- **Recruit users manually**: You can't wait for users to come to you; founders resist out of shyness and laziness
- **Give early users an insanely great experience**: Delight a small number of users rather than a mediocre one for many
- **Learn from manual work**: Pebble assembled its first watches itself and learned things it would never have known otherwise
- **The Stripe example ("Collison installation")**: When anyone agreed to try Stripe, the founders said "Right then, give me your laptop" and set them up on the spot
- **Consult**: YC sometimes advises B2B founders to pick a single user and act as consultants building just for them until they fit that user's needs; you'll usually find you've made something other users want too
