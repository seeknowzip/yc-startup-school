# Module 3 workbook

Use only when the user requests guided practice, a self-assessment, or a workbook. A module invocation alone does not require an interview.

Use the questions below as a bank, adapting them to the user's stage and already supplied answers. In a guided conversation, ask a manageable question and wait; use an available question interface or ordinary chat. Offering a few answer options can make a question easier to answer. After each answer, respond briefly and connect it to the relevant lecture before moving on. If the user wants the complete worksheet, provide it together. Skip irrelevant or answered questions, and respect requests to stop or change focus.

Read the relevant original material for the concepts used. Separate the user's answers, source-grounded observations, and your interpretation. Summarize supported strengths, gaps, and a useful next action when the requested exercise is complete. Do not invent a readiness score; if a score is requested, explain the rubric, evidence, and uncertainty.

Save only when a file is part of the user's request. Use their destination; if they ask to save without one, use a `yc-startup-school-notes/` folder in the current working directory with a descriptive dated filename. Preserve existing files and distinguish partial answers from a completed exercise. Otherwise finish in conversation.

## Question bank

1. Who would you want as a co-founder — have you actually asked them, rather than assuming they're unavailable? If one says no, who would they pick? (Harj Taggar)
2. Describe a stressful situation you've been through with your potential co-founder. How did they handle it? (Harj Taggar)
3. Have you explicitly discussed why each of you wants to start, what success looks like, how much you'll work, and how long each of you can go without a salary? (Harj Taggar; Catheryn Li & Divya Bhat)
4. What's your current equity split — could each co-founder stay motivated in year 3 when things are hard? (Michael Seibel)
5. What vesting, cliff, and departure arrangements have you agreed on, and why do they fit your team? (Michael Seibel)
6. Is your founding team the smallest number of people who can build an MVP, get it to customers, and start learning? (Michael Seibel)
7. What reasons support your equity split — rewarding past contributions such as the idea or an earlier start, or motivating the work still to be done? (Michael Seibel)
8. When did you last have a frank 1-on-1 with your co-founder about how the relationship is going? (Harj Taggar; Catheryn Li & Divya Bhat)
