### Finding a Co-Founder (Harj Taggar)
- **Why you need one**: More work done, emotional support, pattern matching. Facebook, Apple — both had co-founders
- **Solo-founder exception** (Harj: in about 90% of cases focus on finding a co-founder; this is the biggest of the ~10% exceptions): (1) a high-conviction idea backed by domain expertise AND (2) you are an engineer who can build it — then build the first version and bring on a co-founder as you get progress and traction
- **What to look for**: Stress tolerance first (most important), aligned high-level goals, complementary trajectory over current skills
- **Where to find**: People you already know first — never assume they're unavailable; always ask. Also: YC co-founder matching, hackathons, open source
- **3 breakup causes**: Loss of mutual respect / both wanting CEO title / different work ethic expectations
- **Maintenance**: Regular 1-on-1s, small pressure releases vs. one big confrontation

### Keys to Successful Co-Founder Relationships (Catheryn Li & Divya Bhat)
- Discuss expectations before committing: how much you'll work (evenings, weekends, vacations), availability and response times, how long each can go without salary or benefits, and what each needs to see by which milestones to keep going
- Keep decisions moving: naming one CEO ("none of this co-CEO stuff") helps break ties; set clear areas of ownership and a final decision-maker per decision type; decision-by-consensus led to gridlock in Divya's first startup
- Hold regularly scheduled one-on-ones with two-way feedback, even if you talk all the time

### Equity (Michael Seibel)
- **Scope**: Seibel limits this advice to tech software startups expecting VC funding, at the pre-product-market-fit beginning; he calls applying it to later-stage or non-tech companies a misuse
- **Core principle**: Be generous with co-founder equity — it motivates future work, not rewards past contribution
- **Typical advice: close to equal splits** (need not be exactly equal): Think about what keeps a co-founder motivated in years two to four, not what they'll accept today
- **Vesting and cliff example to verify and contextualize**: 4-year vesting, 1-year cliff (Seibel: "extremely typical"; he says vesting and cliffs should apply to all founders) — discuss applicability to the actual team and jurisdiction
- **Smallest founding team**: Minimum people who can build MVP + get it to customers + start learning
- **CEO decision rights**: The lecture says the CEO should reserve the right to fire non-performing founders; examine it in context of the actual governance arrangement
- **Pre-PMF breakup guidelines** (what YC has seen work; "perhaps you should consider" discussing them): a co-founder who leaves or is fired before the 1-year cliff → typically a token 2-5%. After cliff but pre-PMF → Seibel recommends retaining no more than 5%
- **Bad reasons for very unequal splits**: "My co-founder agreed" / "I had the idea" / "I started six months earlier" / "My co-founder needs a salary and I don't" / "I'm older and more experienced" / "I hired my co-founder after raising money" / "I hired my co-founder post-launch"
